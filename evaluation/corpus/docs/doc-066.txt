BOTTOM LINE
Dr. Krishnamurthy concedes he did not obtain a critical view of safety before
clipping, did not convert to open, and did not personally see the patient on
POD 2 despite two calls from nursing. He blames the delay on the hospitalist
and on the family's refusal of blood. He was a poor witness after lunch. The
alcohol-treatment history came in over objection and he was visibly rattled.

1. BACKGROUND AND CREDENTIALS (pp. 6-41)

Born in Chennai; came to the United States in 1998; naturalized U.S. citizen since 2009
(11:4-12). Medical school abroad, residency at a university program in
Shreveport. Louisiana license no. MD.204417; NPI 1740388215; DEA registration
FK4471826 (14:2-15:9). Board certified in general surgery, recertified 2019.
Joined Bayou Teche Surgical Associates, APMC in August 2012; became a shareholder in
2016. Senior partner is Dr. Gerald Broussard, whom everyone calls "Gerry" (22:1).

He performs "roughly 150 to 180" laparoscopic cholecystectomies a year (CPT 47562).
He admitted one prior bile duct injury, in 2018, which was "recognized on the
table and repaired" (27:14-29:3). That claim, he agreed, was settled by his
carrier in 2019 for $350,000 without a panel opinion (30:6-22). Ms. Nwosu-Hebert
objected; instruction not to answer was withdrawn after we cited La. C.C.P. art. 1443.

Insurance: Pelican Physicians Mutual Insurance Co., policy no. PPM-LA-0092817, limits
$1 million per claim. He is a qualified provider under La. R.S. 40:1231.8, so
the cap applies. He does not know whether Pelican has reserved the claim (33:1-9).

Health and fitness for duty (34:12-41:20). Over a continuing objection he
testified that he completed a 90-day residential program for alcohol dependence in
early 2021 under a monitoring agreement with the state physicians' health program,
that the agreement ran five years, and that it was still in force on the date
of surgery. He denies any relapse. He agreed that his privileges at
Our Lady of the Bayou Medical Center were "voluntarily restricted" for eleven weeks in 2021.
He takes naltrexone 50 mg daily (39:7). He said random screens were all negative; we
have not seen them. FOLLOW UP: subpoena the monitoring file.

2. THE PATIENT (pp. 42-67)

Clarence J. Thibodeaux Jr., known as "Boo," DOB 04/02/1958, hospital MRN OLB-00381127,
of 1187 Bayou Tortue Road, Youngsville, LA 70518. Retired after 31 years as a
machinist at Atchafalaya Pipe & Supply Co.; Army veteran. History of type 2 diabetes,
atrial fibrillation on apixaban, and BMI of 37 (Exhibit 7, H&P). The witness first
saw Mr. Thibodeaux in clinic on January 6, 2025 for symptomatic gallstones on referral
from his PCP.

The witness knew from the intake sheet that Mr. Thibodeaux and his wife are
Jehovah's Witnesses and that he carried a signed refusal of blood products
(48:3-49:17). He agreed the refusal covered whole blood and red cells but not
albumin or cell salvage, and that he "did not get into those details" at the
consent visit. He did not call the liaison committee at the St. Landry Parish Kingdom Hall
although the family asked him to (51:2).

Consent was signed January 20, 2025. Apixaban was held 48 hours. He does not recall
discussing conversion to open or bile duct injury specifically; the form lists
both (53:10-56:1).

3. THE OPERATION -- JANUARY 23, 2025 (pp. 68-121)

Surgery at OLB main OR, room 6, in-room 7:42 a.m., incision 8:09, close 9:31.
Assisted by a first-year PA whose name he could not recall. No cholangiogram:
"I do them selectively" (74:8). He described "a fair amount of inflammation" in
Calot's triangle and agreed that his operative note does not use the words
"critical view" (79:1-81:15). He placed two clips on what he believed was the
cystic duct and divided it. Shown the still images (Landry Exhibits 11-14), he
agreed that the structure "could be" the common duct and that its diameter on
image 13 "looks larger than I remembered" (88:20-90:6).

He agreed with Dr. Howard Weiss's report that the injury is a Strasberg class E2
transection, but not that it was below the standard of care: "it is a known
complication" (97:3). Asked why he did not convert or call Dr. Broussard, who
was operating next door: "In hindsight I would have" (101:12).

EHR audit log (Exhibit 19). The operative note was created at 9:58 a.m. under
user ID akrishnamurthy2 from workstation OR6-WS02, IP 10.44.6.112, and was amended
at 6:14 a.m. on 1/26/2025, after the transfer, from a remote session at
198.51.100.23. The amendment added the sentence "anatomy clearly identified."
He says he was "cleaning up dictation" from home (112:5-118:22). This is the
best material in the transcript.

4. POST-OPERATIVE COURSE (pp. 122-198)

Discharged home same day at 1:15 p.m. Returned to the ED the next evening,
January 24, with pain and vomiting; admitted to the hospitalist service under
Dr. Simone Fontenot-Reyes. Labs that night: WBC 19.4, total bilirubin 4.8,
lactate 3.1 (Exhibit 22).

Nurse Tran (Kim-Ly Tran, RN) charted calls to the witness's cell, 337-555-0181, at
10:52 p.m. and 2:17 a.m. He remembers one call, not two, and says he ordered a
HIDA scan "for the morning" (131:4-134:9). His phone records (Exhibit 24) show
both calls connected, 3 min and 1 min. He was not on call; Gerry was. He did
not come in. He first examined the patient at about 11:30 a.m. on January 25,
some 26 hours after readmission (140:2).

By then the patient was septic, with a hemoglobin of 6.9. The family, through
Mrs. Thibodeaux and their daughter Monique Thibodeaux-Arceneaux, declined transfusion. The
witness testified that "if they had let us give blood he would be alive"
(152:11). On cross by Mr. Landry he agreed that the bleeding followed the
bile leak, that the leak followed the transection, and that no one offered
cell salvage or erythropoietin (155:1-158:16). He conceded Dr. Fontenot-Reyes
asked him twice for a surgical decision before ERCP was arranged.

Transfer to a tertiary center in New Orleans was requested at 4:40 p.m. on
the 25th and accepted at 9:05 p.m.; the patient left by ground at 1:20 a.m.
He died on January 29, 2025 of biliary sepsis and multi-organ failure (death
certificate, Exhibit 31). He was 66.

The witness did not attend the funeral and has not spoken with the family.
He learned from Earline's pastor -- he corrected himself, "her elder" -- that
the decedent's mother, Odile Thibodeaux, who is 92, had been living with the
couple and depended on Clarence's pension (171:9). Monique now cares for her.
