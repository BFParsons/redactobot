Thank you for your instructions. I examined Mr Kinnaird on 4.11.25 for 50 minutes and have read his GP records from Eden Vale Surgery (Dr Meera Sivanesan), the hospital notes and the employer's RIDDOR report. This report is addressed to the Court and complies with CPR Part 35.

1. The accident. Mr Kinnaird, a quarry plant operator with Solway Aggregates Ltd for nine years, was struck on the right shoulder and thrown from an access platform when a conveyor guard gave way. He fell about 2.5 metres.

2. Injuries. He sustained a displaced fracture of the right clavicle, treated by plate fixation on 21 February, fractures of the 6th to 8th right ribs and a scaphoid fracture of the right wrist that was not identified until April. He is right-handed.

3. Progress. The clavicle has united. The scaphoid went on to non-union and he had a bone graft and screw fixation on 2 July 2025. He remains off work. He takes co-codamol 30/500 up to eight a day and amitriptyline 25 mg at night.

4. Past history. The GP records show an episode of low mood in 2019 following the death of his father, treated with citalopram for six months, and attendances for gout. Nothing relevant to the right arm. He told me he drinks "four or five pints most nights since the accident"; his GP recorded alcohol intake of 50 units a week in September and has referred him to the community service. I mention this because it bears on the bone healing and on the prognosis.

5. Present complaints. Pain and stiffness at the wrist; cannot grip a spanner; wakes most nights. He is anxious about money, having moved onto Universal Credit in August, and his partner is expecting their third child in January.

6. Examination. Healed 11 cm scar over the clavicle. Shoulder movements full. Right wrist: flexion 30 degrees, extension 25 (left 70/70). Grip strength 14 kg right, 46 kg left.

7. Opinion and prognosis. On the balance of probabilities all three fractures were caused by the index accident. The delay in diagnosing the scaphoid fracture has, in my opinion, materially worsened the outcome; you may wish to take separate advice on that. I expect permanent loss of about half of wrist movement. He will not return to plant operation. There is a 20 to 25 per cent risk of wrist arthritis requiring fusion within 15 years, at a present-day private cost of about £9,500.

8. I would place the wrist injury within the "significant permanent disability" bracket of the Judicial College Guidelines.
