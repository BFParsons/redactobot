Privacy policy and terms of use

Effective 14 November 2026. Applies to the Redactobot web app and the Redactobot Chrome extension, version 0.3.

This is a technology demonstration. Do not enter real sensitive data.

Redactobot was built for a hackathon to show an idea. It is not a validated privacy, security or compliance tool, it has not been independently audited, and it will miss things. Please try it with fictional or non-sensitive text only. If you choose to use it with real information, you do so entirely at your own risk.

The short version

  • The text you paste or open is checked in your browser, on your device. We do not receive it.
  • We have no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking cookies. We do not sell or share data, because we do not collect any.
  • One optional feature, cloud cleanup, sends text to Google. It is off unless you start it, it shows you the exact text first, and that text has had the detected identifiers replaced. Anything the detection missed goes with it.
  • Only your settings are saved, on your device. Your text and the list of real values behind each placeholder are kept in memory and are gone when you close or reload the page.
  • Removing names does not make text anonymous. Always read the result yourself. You remain responsible for what you share.

1. What Redactobot is

Redactobot is an open-source demonstration project, published under the Apache License 2.0 and built as an entry for the #BuildHybridAI 2026 hackathon. It finds details such as names, contact details, account numbers and dates in text, and replaces them with placeholders before the text is shared with an AI chatbot.

It is offered free of charge by the project's author ("we", "us") as a demonstration. It is not a commercial product or a professional service, it comes with no support commitment, and it may change, break or be withdrawn at any time without notice.

2. Do not enter sensitive data

Please do not use Redactobot with information that would cause harm if it were exposed. That includes, for example:

  • real client, patient, customer, employee or student records;
  • information covered by legal privilege, professional secrecy or a confidentiality agreement;
  • health, financial, immigration, criminal-record or other special-category information about real people;
  • government identifiers, passwords, keys or other credentials;
  • classified, export-controlled or otherwise legally restricted information.

The sample documents built into the app are fictional and are the intended way to try it.

3. What happens to your text

When you paste text or open a file in the web app:

  • The file is read by your browser. It is not uploaded.
  • Detection runs on your device, using pattern rules in the page and, for names, places and sensitive topics, Gemini Nano, the AI model built into Chrome. Redactobot asks for that model to be used on-device only and has no cloud fallback for this step.
  • Your text, the detections and the table linking each placeholder (for example [PERSON_1]) to its real value are held in the page's memory. They are not written to storage and are not sent to us. They are discarded when you close or reload the page.

Chrome itself downloads and runs the on-device model. How Chrome and Google handle the model, its download and any related diagnostics is governed by Google's own terms and privacy policy, not by this one. We cannot verify or control what the browser does internally.

4. The optional cloud step

The web app and the extension include an optional second step, cloud cleanup (and, in the web app, asking a question about the cleaned text). This step is not required. If you use it:

  • The text that is sent is the version with detected identifiers already replaced by placeholders, together with any question you typed. The web app shows you the exact text before anything is sent.
  • It is sent to Google's Gemini models through Firebase AI Logic, a Google service, protected by Firebase App Check. Google processes it under its own terms. We do not control how long Google keeps it or how Google uses it.
  • The placeholder table, file names and your original text are not sent.
  • Any identifying detail that detection missed is still in that text and will be sent. This is the main reason not to use real sensitive data.
  • Other sensitive facts that are not identifiers, such as a medical condition, are deliberately left in the text at this stage, because removing them is what the cloud step is for.

The response comes back to your browser. We do not operate a server that stores your requests or the answers.

5. The Chrome extension

The extension adds a Redactobot switch under the message box on chatgpt.com, claude.ai and gemini.google.com. The switch starts off each time one of those pages loads.

  • When the switch is off, the extension does not read what you paste.
  • When the switch is on, the extension reads text you paste or drop into the message box, checks it on your device, and inserts the cleaned version. It does not read what you type, the rest of the page, your conversation history, or files you upload. It never presses Send.
  • Text being checked is held in the extension's memory until the paste finishes, is cancelled, or expires (at most ten minutes), and is then discarded.
  • Cloud cleanup in the extension is off by default. If you turn it on in the extension's settings, text that passes the local check is sent to Google as described in section 4, without a preview for each paste.
  • The extension uses Chrome's storage permission to save your settings and its offscreen permission to run the on-device model. It can contact only Google's Firebase services, and only for the optional cloud step.

Whatever ends up in the message box is then handled by that chatbot's provider (OpenAI, Anthropic or Google) under their terms once you send it. Redactobot has no control over, and takes no responsibility for, what those services do with it.

9. Limits of redaction

  • Detection is imperfect. In our own small tests on fictional documents it caught most, not all, names and identifiers. Your text will differ from our tests. Assume something has been missed.
  • Redacted text can still identify people. A job title, a rare condition, a small town or the combination of ordinary facts can point to one person even with every name removed.
  • It is not a compliance tool. The lists named after HIPAA, GDPR, CCPA, GLBA, PCI DSS and other rules show what each rule mentions. Redactobot does not determine, and never claims, that any text complies with any law, regulation, court rule, professional rule or contract.
  • The FOIA release mode is a formatting aid. The exemption codes it applies are defaults for a trained person to review. They are not a legal determination that an exemption applies.
  • It does not read everything. Scanned pages, images, typed text in chat boxes and file uploads to chat sites are not checked.
  • Nothing here is legal, compliance, security or other professional advice.

11. Security

The design keeps your text on your device and limits the page and the extension to contacting Google's Firebase services only. The source code is public so that anyone can check this. Even so, no software is free of defects, this project has not had a professional security review, and other software on your device, including other browser extensions, may be able to see what is on the page. We make no promise that Redactobot is secure.

12. Your choices and deleting data

  • Do not use the cloud step if you do not want any text to leave your device.
  • Web app: close or reload the page to discard your text. To remove saved settings, clear this site's data in your browser's settings.
  • Extension: switch it off in a tab, turn off individual sites in its settings, or remove the extension from Chrome, which deletes its saved settings.
  • On-device model: managed by Chrome; see Chrome's settings and Google's documentation.
